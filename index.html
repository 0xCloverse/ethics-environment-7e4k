Engineers and the Environment

한태영 · Engineering Ethics · Oct. 26, 2026
Big question

Engineers pollute the land, air and rivers. Engineers also clean them up. So what do we owe the environment?

Q1 Sustainable development

Use it, but leave enough

Meet present needs without hurting nature's capacity to absorb our impact, and without hurting future generations' ability to meet their own needs.ASCE definition, Harris ch. 9
In one line

Don't use up what nature and our grandchildren will need.

Why it matters

Codes now look past today's client to nature and future people. NSPE III.2.d and ASCE both name it.

Its limit

Codes say engineers "should strive to" follow it. A recommendation, not a duty, so it is hard to enforce.

Q3 Who has value?

Anthropocentric vs. non-anthropocentric

Anthropocentric · 인간중심
Only humans have value in themselves.

Nature is instrumental: it matters because it is useful to us.

Save the forest? For wood, clean air and recreation.
Non-anthropocentric · 비인간중심
Nature has value in itself, too.

Some natural things have intrinsic value.

Save the forest? Because the forest deserves respect.

Aldo Leopold: "land ethic"

We are members of one "biotic community" with soil, water, plants and animals. Right = keeping it whole, stable and beautiful.

Textbook: a "modified" view

Respect nature for its own sake, but a big enough human good can justify harm. Blind salamanders vs. many human lives.

Q4 Why is it rather fair?

The sky is one shared sink

Everyone pours waste into the same sink. It looked endless, so nobody worried. Rich countries poured in most of it. Now it is nearly full.

1

Polluter pays

"You broke it, you fix it." Who filled the sink should clean first.

2

Common but differentiated

Everyone shares the problem; richer, more responsible nations lead.

3

Right to develop

Poor countries still need room to grow out of poverty.

Singer borrows John Locke: take only if you leave "enough and as good" for others

Case NSPE Board of Ethical Review · Case 21-2

Plan for a climate that hasn't come yet?

The job

Engineer A designs an access road across a tidal saltmarsh for a new health care facility.

The rules

Local rules use past weather only. Nothing about sea level rise.

The worry

The road could make 20 upstream homes unlivable 10+ years sooner. A asks for a costly flood study.

The client

"Skip it, unless regulators ask."

Board's ruling
Engineer A must consider climate impacts on the public, even if no law requires it.
1

Talk to the client: risks, fixes, and the client's own risk of skipping it.

2

Refused? Put the concern in an engineering report for regulators and the public.

3

Refused again? Withdraw from the project.

NSPE Code I.1 · II.3.a · III.2.d (sustainable development)
